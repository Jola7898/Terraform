Camera calibration

lens not calibrated

The drone's wide-angle lens bends straight lines, and VGGT (a pinhole-camera model) turns that into bent walls and opened-up corners. Calibrate it once: print a checkerboard, hold it flat in front of the camera and move it slowly around the view, near and far, tilted, and into every corner, until the grid below fills. Every take recorded afterwards carries the calibration, and its frames are straightened before reconstruction.

Capture

      
Three ways in, one pipeline

      
Every source ends up as the same thing - a session of timestamped frames - so the same reconstruction, viewer and export tools work for all of them.

      

        
📱
Phone app

          
Android app streams camera, IMU and GPS to the Studio over LAN, Tailscale or an HTTPS tunnel. Reconstruct live while recording, or after.

        
🛸
Drone link

          
RTSP video and MAVLink telemetry straight from the drone. Fisheye lens calibration, indoor and outdoor modes, GPS georeferencing.

        
🎞
Video upload

          
Drop in an mp4, or a .zip of image frames or of a video. Optional fisheye correction, image enhancement and motion-based frame selection before reconstruction.

      

    

  


  

    

      

        

          
Capture paths

          
Fly it the way the model wants to be seen

          
Orbits and lawn-mower passes give every surface several views from different angles. RTVIO picks the frames for you from how the camera actually moved, so a slow orbit is not thinned out and a hover is not counted twice.

          
    
            
  • Keep the subject in frame and move smoothly - no need to stop at each position.
    • 
            
  • Overlap generously; the more views of a surface, the cleaner it fuses.
    • 
            
  • Loop back over your start point when you can.
    • 
          

        

        
      

    

  


  
  

    

      
Methodology

      
From frames to a mesh

      
Seven stages, each one a small, testable step. Nothing here needs COLMAP, a sparse map or camera calibration up front.

      

        
Select frames
Lucas-Kanade tracking measures how far the view moved. A frame is kept only when the camera has really travelled, so a 30 fps clip becomes well-spaced views.

        
Straighten the lens
Wide-angle drone footage is remapped to an ideal pinhole image (fisheye calibration or a field-of-view estimate), because VGGT assumes a pinhole camera.

        
Predict geometry
VGGT-1B reads a window of up to 64 frames at once and outputs camera poses and a depth map with confidence for every frame.

        
Filter
Low-confidence pixels, depth edges and grazing normals are dropped on the GPU before anything is fused.

        
Chain the windows
Neighbouring windows share frames. A robust Sim(3) fit over those overlaps aligns each window to the last, correcting scale as well as pose.

        
Fuse
Points from all windows go into one voxel accumulator that tracks colour, normal and how many frames saw each point. Points seen only once are removed.

        
Mesh & export
Screened Poisson surface reconstruction, distance trim and island removal. Export PLY, GLB, LAS or a COLMAP model; optional GPS alignment to real coordinates.

      

    

  


  

    

      
    

  


  
  

    

      
Architecture

      
System overview

      
Reconstruction is treated as inference, not optimisation: a feed-forward transformer predicts cameras and depth for a whole window of frames at once, and a small amount of classical geometry ties the windows together.


      

        
Feed-forward geometryNo feature matching or bundle adjustment. One VGGT-1B pass gives poses, intrinsics and depth for up to 64 frames.

        
Windowed, then chainedOverlapping windows are aligned by a robust Sim(3) fit, so length is limited by time, not by GPU memory.

        
Always-warm workerThe model stays resident on the GPU; frame selection and attention kernels are tuned for a fast turn-around.

Why RTVIO

      
Compared with COLMAP and classical pipelines

      
COLMAP is an excellent, well-proven tool and still the accuracy reference in good conditions. RTVIO trades a little of that peak precision for speed, robustness and a live workflow. We have not run COLMAP head-to-head on this clip, so the table describes how the methods behave, not measured numbers.

      

        

          
AspectCOLMAP · classical SfM / MVSRTVIO

          
            
MethodDetect features, match image pairs, incremental structure-from-motion, bundle adjustment, then PatchMatch dense stereo.A feed-forward transformer predicts poses and depth for whole windows of frames; windows are chained with a robust Sim(3).

            
Speed on videoslowerMatching and bundle adjustment grow with the number of frames; hundreds of frames usually take many minutes to hours.fasterRoughly linear in windows. 1033 frames in 102 s here, and a new job starts with the model already loaded.

            
Low texture, glass, nightfragileNeeds distinctive features; blank walls, reflections and dark scenes cause failed matches and holes.robustLearned depth priors fill weak areas - reflective glass and blank walls still come out as surfaces.

            
Small baseline, rotation, blurfragileInitialisation needs parallax; pure rotation and motion blur reduce matches.tolerantWorks from smooth handheld or drone motion; frame selection removes near-duplicates.

            
Live / streamingofflineBatch process: capture first, reconstruct afterwards.liveReconstructs window by window while a phone or drone is still recording, with a live 3D view.

            
Peak accuracystrongerSub-pixel bundle adjustment on high-resolution, well-textured, high-overlap images is still the reference.lowerVGGT sees frames at ≈518 px wide; fine geometry is limited by that and by drift across chained windows.

            
Scale & georeferencetieRelative scale unless you add control points or GPS priors.tieRelative scale from video alone; with GPS a Sim(3) fit gives metric ENU coordinates.

            
Lens handlingCalibrates camera models inside bundle adjustment.Needs a pinhole image, so wide-angle lenses are corrected first (checkerboard calibration or a field-of-view estimate).

            
HardwareSfM runs on CPU; dense stereo needs a CUDA GPU.Needs a CUDA GPU with roughly 4 GB or more; more VRAM allows longer windows.

          
        

      

      
Use COLMAP when you have a high-resolution, well-textured photo set and time to spare. Use RTVIO when you have video, want results in minutes, or want to watch the model grow while you fly.
